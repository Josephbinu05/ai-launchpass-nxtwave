# AI LaunchPass — five-slide growth plan

**Challenge:** 500 final-year engineering students · seven days · ₹2,000 · free “Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes” workshop.

This is a proposed campaign, not an executed one. All contributions below are assumptions. The content fits five slides; the timeline belongs on slide 2.

## Slide 1 — Make the workshop relevant, then distribute it

**Main message:** Students need a concrete outcome; a ₹2,000 campaign needs trusted campus reach.

- Final-year students need a practical portfolio starting point, not another generic webinar.
- Hook: **“What AI project could you build in 60 minutes?”**
- Three questions connect branch, experience and interests to a beginner-friendly project.
- Prioritize campus relationships and referrals; paid acquisition is not the primary engine.

**Suggested visual:** `Personal relevance → Registration → Campus distribution`, with the 500 / ₹2,000 / 7-day constraints beneath it.

## Slide 2 — Three engines, one seven-day operating plan

**Main message:** A 360-student direct base can seed a 144-registration referral contribution.

- **Captains: 240.** Recruit 20 student leaders; equip each with a link and a target of 12 unique direct registrations.
- **Clubs/groups: 120.** Work with AI/coding clubs, IEEE chapters and placement-group admins; use permitted, relevant posts.
- **Referrals: 144 modeled.** Give every registered student a personal pass, useful resource milestones and campus progress.

**Suggested visual:** Three contribution bars beside this seven-day timeline:

| Day | Concrete action / decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Shortlist 60 potential leaders across eight campuses; ask club admins for distribution permission. |
| 2 | Onboard 20 captains; issue links, a demo and one share message; confirm club posting slots. |
| 3 | Launch: each captain targets three registrations; clubs target their first 40; inspect matcher-to-registration drop-off. |
| 4 | Repeat the captain target; second 40 from clubs; improve one weak step using observed drop-off. |
| 5 | Repeat captain target; final 40 from clubs; prompt opted-in registrants to invite relevant friends. |
| 6 | Complete 12 per captain; compare channels and referral conversion; add captains or club reach if below the modeled path. |
| 7 | Final permitted reminders; deduplicate/verify registrations before ranking rewards; report 500-target progress and attendance follow-up plan. |

## Slide 3 — AI LaunchPass gives every registration a next step

**Main message:** The working asset connects student value to the distribution strategy.

- Deterministic project matching shows a realistic guided build, stack and learning outcomes.
- Registration creates a demo pass with a unique referral code and a ready-to-share link.
- Prototype milestones and a campus leaderboard make contribution visible.
- The Growth Command Center shows acquisition sources, funnel conversion and the gap to 500.

**Suggested visual:** Five app panels: `Project match → Register → LaunchPass → Invite friends → Campus progress`. Add a clear **Simulation / browser-local prototype** label.

## Slide 4 — 504 is an assumption to test

**Main message:** The simulator exposes both the path and its narrow margin.

- Direct base: `20 × 12 + 120 = 360`. Organic contribution: **0**.
- One referral generation: `360 × 40% sharing × 2.5 qualified invites × 40% conversion = 144`; total **504**.
- At **30% conversion**, total falls to **468**. Recover through three additional captains at the same productivity, or about 25 additional club registrations; validate before scaling.
- Proposed incentive allocation: **₹1,000 / ₹600 / ₹400**, total **₹2,000**, for the top three verified captain contributors. Proposed resource milestones have no extra cash allocation.

**Suggested visual:** Simulator at 504, then at 468. Footer: **Unique registrations assumed; no overlap, recurring referral generations or guaranteed outcome.** Cost per target registration is ₹4 if the full budget is spent and 500 are reached; it is a target, not an observed CPA.

## Slide 5 — Ship the loop, then learn from the constraint

**Main message:** The deliverable makes a growth hypothesis measurable and easy to challenge.

- Built: student journey, referral pass, campus rankings, growth dashboard and interactive simulator.
- Design change: expand the landing-page concept to include distribution, attribution and a clear next action.
- Judgment: choose a rules-based matcher over a chatbot; focus on three channels rather than a broad list.
- Next test: 10–15 student usability sessions, two invitation hooks and validation of reward motivation. These tests have not yet been run.

**Suggested visual:** Student pass beside the growth simulator. Show the actual deployed URL or local prototype during presentation. AI examples in the supporting notes are explicitly reconstructed from the supplied brief, not claimed historical transcripts.
