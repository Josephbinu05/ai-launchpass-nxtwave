# AI LaunchPass — demo video script

**Target runtime: 2:50. Hard stop: 3:00.** The script is roughly 320 spoken words, allowing time for clicks. Record the working product; keep this script off-screen. A finished recording is still a separate submission item.

## Before recording

- Use a fresh local demo state or **Reset Demo Data**. Leave the simulator at its defaults.
- Prepare fictional details: Riya Demo, `riya.demo@example.com`, `9000000001`, VIT, CSE / IT, graduation year 2027. The form is a prototype; use no real student contact details.
- Practice choosing **CSE / IT → Beginner → Career / Resume**. Show whichever project the matcher actually recommends; the wording below does not depend on its title.
- Keep notifications off. Use a readable viewport and show the app's simulation label. Do not cut away to a real messaging conversation.

## 0:00–0:22 — The constraint

**Screen:** Landing page, with the workshop hook and demo label visible.

**Say:**

“The challenge is 500 final-year engineering students, seven days and ₹2,000. My starting point is that a useful workshop still needs distribution. AI LaunchPass connects a relevant project idea to registration, student referrals and campus participation. This is a simulation, and the dashboard numbers are synthetic.”

## 0:22–0:59 — Student value

**Screen:** Start the project matcher. Choose branch, experience and interest. Pause on the result. Open registration and enter the prepared fictional details.

**Say:**

“The student answers three questions about their branch, experience and interests. The matcher recommends a guided AI project, with a build time, a stack and clear learning outcomes. It uses a local rules-based library, so there is no API setup. That gives the student a concrete reason to join. I can now register with demo details.”

## 0:59–1:30 — The referral loop

**Screen:** Submit registration, show the LaunchPass and copy the referral link. Point to the WhatsApp button, milestones and campus leaderboard.

**Say:**

“Registration creates this personal pass and referral link. The student can copy it or prepare a WhatsApp invitation. Milestones and campus progress create a reason to bring friends. These rewards are campaign concepts, not promises. This prototype stores everything in this browser; a real campaign would need a shared database and verified referral attribution.”

## 1:30–1:57 — Growth operations

**Screen:** Switch to **Growth Team View**. Show goal progress, funnel, channel breakdown and campus rankings. Avoid lingering on every number.

**Say:**

“The Growth Command Center shows the registration goal, conversion funnel, source mix and campus performance. My demo registration updates local totals. The team can see where the journey loses students and which distribution sources contribute. The proposed spend is ₹1,000, ₹600 and ₹400 for the top three verified campus captains.”

## 1:57–2:25 — Challenge the model

**Screen:** Open the simulator. Point to **504**, then change referral conversion from **40% to 30%**. Show **468**, the **32-person gap** and calculated recommendations.

**Say:**

“Twenty captains delivering twelve registrations each, plus 120 from clubs, gives a base of 360. Forty percent sharing, two-point-five qualified invites and forty percent conversion adds 144 referrals: 504 total. But lower conversion to thirty percent and we fall to 468. The gap is visible immediately. These are testable assumptions, not guaranteed results.”

## 2:25–2:40 — Judgment and learning

**Screen:** Keep the simulator and recommendations visible; optionally return to the project result.

**Say:**

“The design moved beyond a landing page to include distribution. I chose a simple matcher over a chatbot and focused on three channels. The next step is to test the hook with students and replace assumptions with observed conversion.”

## 2:40–2:50 — Close

**Screen:** Student/Growth mode toggle and product name.

**Say:**

“The goal is a small growth system that makes the 500-registration strategy measurable, testable and ready to improve.”
